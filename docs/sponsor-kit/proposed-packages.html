The recommendation

Sell in this order

1. Best in Show, $1,600, to one anchor business.

The presenting sponsor is the only placement that reaches past the contest’s own pages, into the article widget in WPR stories and the daily-newsletter contest block. Sell it first and don’t split it. A business with a natural tie to pets is the obvious fit, but any business that wants broad local goodwill works.

2. Blue Ribbon, $1,200, as the second sale.

Gallery plus bracket gives one business the two pages readers browse, across the whole contest. If no one wants both, sell them separately at $700 and $500.

3. Double Vote Hours for small budgets.

A single Double Vote Hour ($150, or three for $400) gives a small business a way in. So does a Match Hour ($150 plus the sponsor's own match, paid to the shelter), the best story of the three for a business that wants to be seen giving. Step-up for any Blue Ribbon buyer: a second Double Vote Hour.

  • Why the rate card. A local business already knows what a week on wausaupilotandreview.com costs, and these prices hold up against that. Each weekly website flight is one slot in a rotation of up to four ads. Every contest placement goes to a single business, so each carries a premium of 1.33 to 1.43 times its rotation equivalent. That premium is well under the four-times difference in share of the space, because this is the contest’s first year and there is no traffic history to sell against.
  • Weeks on screen come from the contest run book (CLAUDE.md, “Running a contest”): a one-week entry period, a one-week Open Round, then four two-day bracket rounds, about a week and a half with the hand-run closes between them. The presenting credit and the gallery run all 3.5 weeks. The bracket page starts as the projected bracket in the Open Round (2.5 weeks).
  • Double Vote Hour is priced at a week of run of site, $150, as the entry point. It is one hour, but the credit appears on every contest page, in the vote-pack picker, in the article widget and in a newsletter block that goes out within the 36 hours before or during the hour, plus a heads-up bar the day before.
  • Match Hour is priced like a Double Vote Hour: its bar shows on every contest page for the hour, and the shelter tally names the sponsor for the rest of the contest. The match itself is the sponsor’s gift to the shelter, on top of the fee, capped at an amount the sponsor chooses; the admin desk shows it on the counter and lists it on the payout statement for the sponsor to settle. It never changes a vote.
  • The live finale now carries the presenting sponsor’s name (“The finale · presented by”) on the countdown card and while it is live. It is part of the presenting placement, so Best in Show’s price is unchanged; it is a reason to buy it.
  • Not for sale: the buyer’s thank-you screen carries no advertising (decided Oct. 23, 2026).
  • Sponsorship is WPR’s revenue. The shelter’s share applies to vote pack sales only, so none of these figures is split with Moments Foster Care. The pitch says so plainly.
  • Standard inventory still sells on its own terms. A sponsor that wants traffic to its own site can add regular WPR placements at published rates: run of site $150/week, a daily AM or PM newsletter slot $300/week, the weekly events newsletter $200/week. Those are rotations, or one of two slots in the events newsletter, so don’t describe them as exclusive.

Decisions for Chris before selling

  • The prices themselves. Change them here, then on the pitch page, the leave-behind and the email.
  • How many Double Vote Hours to sell, and when. Staff schedule them in the admin desk (Packs & bonuses tab). Each one changes the race for an hour, so the editors should agree on the number.
  • No prizes, so no sprints. WPR’s legal footing (decided Oct. 26, 2026) is that nothing of value goes to any pet’s owner, so a sponsor can buy Double Vote Hours and Match Hours but cannot give a prize. Sprints, which award one, are not sold.
  • How a match is paid. Whether the sponsor pays Moments directly or through WPR, and when, goes in the sponsor’s agreement. Its cap is the most the sponsor can owe.
  • Category exclusivity. It isn’t built in: nothing stops a competitor of the presenting sponsor from buying the gallery. If an anchor sponsor asks for it, decide whether to grant it and what to charge.
  • Category rules. Gaming, alcohol, cannabis and financial-services sponsors may need a disclaimer line; settle that before the slot sells. WPR may decline any sponsorship. Check whether a prospect already advertises with WPR before pitching them.
  • Click reporting. The contest counts clicks on each sponsor placement, without cookies, only once its analytics are switched on, and it does not count impressions. Confirm with the developer before promising a report.
