## Design Specificity Verdict

LLM assessment: the hero (taped polaroids, paw-texture banner, italic "cutest" in Fraunces at optical size 144) and the contest microcopy ("61 votes behind Gus", "the first pet out needs 51 to pass") belong to this product. Below the hero it is a generic kit: about 12 identical white cards on Home, a two-column product grid with 24 identical heavy Vote buttons, a SaaS pricing-tier checkout, a share row, a green-check receipt. It reads as a well-made newspaper civic tool, not a joyful local competition. Rivalry, upsets, town pride, the shelter animals and the crowning exist in the data but never become design.

Deterministic scan: 13 CLI warnings (exit 2): border-accent-on-rounded ×9 (.card, .raised, .award, .modal, .sponsor-card, .stat, .mod-card, .gfx-panel, .w-card), overused-font ×3, side-tab ×1. The browser overlay ran on 6 pages: Home 14 overlays (italic-serif-display on the h1, kicker-above-heading ×3, a pulsing live dot, three top-border cards), the shared chrome on every page (10–10.5px masthead tagline and dateline, a 157-character footer line, cream palette), and the widget 1 (its top-border card). Agreement: the top-border-card rule is the mechanical signature of A's "every block is the same card". False positives: overused-font (fires on the Georgia fallback faces; Fraunces is the specified system), cream-palette (#F6F2E9 is the spec), side-tab (.caption is a pull quote), hero-eyebrow-chip (no chip). The footer line length is real, but the copy is legally required, so only its measure can change. The detector missed the biggest problems: chrome burying the pet, the generic peak moments, and the upsell on the receipt.

## Overall Impression

A trustworthy, accessible, well-engineered contest that looks like a newspaper page about a contest. The single biggest opportunity: make the phone's first screen and the three peak moments (buying votes, an upset, the champion) feel like an event.

## What's Working

- Contest-mechanics microcopy and status: standings, the gap to the cutoff, the closest race, honest payment outcomes. Specific and trust-building.
- Typography with character: Fraunces at optical size 144, the teal italic "cutest", JetBrains Mono counts, and metric-matched fallbacks so nothing reflows. The polaroid hero is the one idea no other product would ship.
- Phase-aware everything, and solid accessibility basics: focus-managed dialog, reduced motion respected, an axe audit at 42 for 42.

## Priority Issues

**[P1] On phones, the chrome buries the pet and the Vote button.** Masthead (~140px) + banner (~200px) + nav + Double Votes bar (~110px) ≈ 500px before content; on a pet page (where share links land) the name is at ~930px and Vote at ~1,290px. Inside the WordPress iframe, WPR's masthead appears twice (App.jsx renders Masthead even when embedded). Fix: hide Masthead when embedded; slim the banner to a ~56px contest bar on interior routes; a one-line bonus bar; photo, name, count and Vote in the first screen of Pet.jsx, plus a sticky bottom "Vote for {name}" bar on phones. Command: /impeccable layout, then /impeccable adapt.

**[P1] The peak moments are generic.** The thank-you screen is a check mark, "Payment received", then about 10 follow-on actions. No upset state exists anywhere (seed 16 leading seed 1 looks like any matchup). The champion reveal is unstaged and repeated on three pages. Fix: a thank-you screen with the pet's photo, its count rolling up when credited, the shelter impact in dollars and one share action; an "Upset alert" state in MatchupCard, the pet page and the widget, plus lead-change events in the ticker; a staged champion reveal with its own warm motif and the narrative up front ("A 16 seed takes the crown"). Command: /impeccable delight, /impeccable animate.

**[P1] Sponsor upsells reach readers at the wrong moments.** Empty slots render "Sponsorship available… Book this placement" (Sponsor.jsx). That's by design, as a revenue lever, but it includes the buyer's thank-you screen, sits above the gallery grid, and uses the same primary button as Vote. Fix: never on the thank-you screen; a quieter, secondary-weight card on reader pages; full upsells on #/sponsors and in ?demo mode. Command: /impeccable quieter.

**[P2] Template monotony and choice overload.** One card treatment everywhere (the detector's 9 top-border-card rules); 24 identical solid Vote buttons outweigh the photos; 5 packs listed from $250 down; 7 share options; 7–10 gallery filters before the first pet. Fix: photo-first cards with a lighter vote affordance and the top three featured large; species chips and sort up front, the rest in a sheet; 4 packs, "More packs" for the rest; rename the "Champion" pack (it collides with the round); "1,200 votes (was 600)". Command: /impeccable bolder, /impeccable distill.

**[P2] The hero shows the wrong pets.** The #1 pet's polaroid sits at the back (.polaroid:nth-child(1) has z-index 1); in the bracket phase the hero shows recent entrants, possibly eliminated ones; a live bracket pet's "More pets" shows eliminated pets. Fix: the leader or champion on the front polaroid; the closest live matchup as the hero face-off during the bracket; "More pets" = the opponent and other live matchups. Command: /impeccable layout.

- Desktop Home: the right column ends early beside the leaderboard.
- Shelters is the flattest page for the cause: generic paw tiles instead of logos, a card inside a card for the featured adoptable, no per-shelter dollars.

## Questions to Consider

- If most traffic is an iPhone opening a pet's share link, why is that page's first screen a newspaper masthead?
- Is this a newspaper page about a contest, or a contest? Which does a newsroom buying it value more?
- What should a 16-over-1 upset look and feel like? Today it looks like a blowout.
- Should the pet photos drive color? They're the only asset that changes every year and every newsroom.
- Would you put "Book this placement" on a reader's receipt in print?
